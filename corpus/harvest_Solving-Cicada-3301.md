If you’re an expert cryptographer interested in solving one of the most complex, creepy, and iconic puzzles in the history of mankind, you may want to give Cicada 3301 a shot. This series of strange online puzzles popped up on a 4Chan message board over a decade ago, and the world has been hooked ever since. The puzzles were originally suggested as a test to recruit brilliant cryptologists for some undisclosed job, but it’s unclear if anyone has been recruited for anything or not. In this article, we’ll take a look at how the first puzzle was solved, what’s involved in solving the remaining puzzles, and what the current state of the project is.
##
Cicada 3301: Overview & Existing Solutions
- Cicada 3301 is a series of extremely complicated online puzzles that were first released on 4Chan in 2012.
- Marcus Wanner solved the first portion of the puzzle, while Joel Eriksson is the most infamous solver for sharing his steps.
- The puzzle remains mostly unsolved, although large portions of it have been solved.
- The last puzzle was released in 2014, and the last message from 3301 occurred in 2017.

Steps
Section 1 of 5:
### Was Cicada 3301 solved?
Download Article
No, although large portions of the puzzle have been solved. Cicada 3301 is technically three different puzzles that are lumped together and treated as one single puzzle, so it depends on how you classify it, but Cicada 3301 as a whole remains unsolved. That said, individual components have been solved, and the first two puzzles released are now considered complete. Marcus Wanner was the first to solve the initial puzzle, while Joel Erikkson is considered the most prolific solver.
Why do some people say it’s eight puzzles? Cicada 3301 can be “split up” in different ways. There were three major runs, one released each year from 2012-2014, but the puzzles can be broken down into smaller stages that are functionally complete puzzles in their own right, where the reward is a link to the next stage.
Is there a solution for the remaining unsolved puzzles? Nobody knows for sure, but considering the fact that many of them have been solved, it seems likely that the unsolved puzzles have a solution of some kind.

Section 2 of 5:
### Who Is Cicada 3301?
Download Article
3301 is the author of the “Cicada” puzzle. The first puzzle image was posted on 4Chan in 2012. 4Chan is an anonymous message board, so beyond the moniker “3301,” nobody has any information about who started this whole thing. Rumors circulated about secret government agencies like the NSA or CIA, Julian Assange, cybersecurity firms, and even the Illuminati. From the best evidence we have, it seems most likely that an independent organization of hackers is responsible.
Section 3 of 5:
### Cicada 3301’s First Puzzle Solution
Download Article
1
The puzzle started with an image posted on 4Chan. On January 4th, 2012, this image was posted on 4Chan’s /x/ (paranormal) board. People initially thought it might have been an ARG or escape-room-style puzzle. People immediately jumped to inspect the image’s text on the page using steganography tools.
2
The image’s code contains a misleading cipher. In the image’s text was the strange phrase {{{1}}}. “Caesar” was a reference to Caesar ciphers, which are a type of letter-shifting puzzle.
This was actually a red herring! The Caesar cipher shifted to a website URL, which led to this hilarious image.
- This is a sign of things to come. The Cicada puzzles contain tons of dead ends, jokes, and strange loops that ultimately don’t lead to the solution.
3
Analyzing the image using software revealed the next clue. Eventually, based on the inclusion of the words “out” and “guess” in the image, someone thought to run the image through OutGuess, a popular decoding tool for cryptography. Using certain settings, people found a hidden URL string that led to this website page.
What’s going on here? The Reddit page links to a subreddit full of encoded posts. The long string of numbers initially looked like something called a “book code,” which is typically a key for a shift cipher. That’s not what it ended up being, though.
4
The image on the strange subreddit contained more clues. Players continued to run the image through more stenography tools to reveal more strings of letters that shifted to more in-text clues and more links.
What happened next? There were dozens and dozens of clues that eventually led to (in no specific order) riddles, coding challenges, PGP keys, ciphers, phone numbers, GPS coordinates, an IRL treasure hunt, a published book that contained a code, a song, hidden TOR servers on the Dark Web, QR Codes, and math puzzles.
5
The solution ended up being… We don’t know! We know for a fact that multiple people solved the puzzle (3301 confirmed as much in an announcement, and there were private logs that were accidentally released on a forum which support that). Marcus Wanner was the first to solve it, but Joel Eriksson is probably the most well-known solver due to how open he has been about his solution process.
How many people have solved the first puzzle? We don’t know for sure, but the total number of solvers probably isn’t very high. If you haven’t figured it out yet, the puzzle is really hard, and you need to know a lot about to even make sense of it all.
An in-depth walkthrough of the solution is published on Joel Eriksson’s blog. A lot of it may not make sense if you aren’t an experienced cryptoanalyst, but it’s fun to look at all the work and effort that went into finding the solution!

Section 4 of 5:
### The Latest Unsolved Puzzle
Download Article
The remaining puzzle surrounds a book called Liber Primus. In 2014, the year after the second puzzle was solved, this image appeared on 4Chan. Fans of Cicada 3301 immediately got to work. They solved enough clues to lead them to Liber Primus, a digital book that contains strange riddles and codes. Many of the individual pages are “solved,” but many of them remain unclear, and the puzzle as a whole has not been completed.
Who wrote Liber Primus? We don’t know. A Slovakian “encyclopedist” is the listed author on an Amazon copy, but since the real Liber Primus was published digitally and anonymously, this is probably a fraudulent copy—especially since it’s missing key pages. Most Cicada players agree the author is just a troll trying to make a quick buck.
How do we know which Cicada puzzles are authentic? The images published by 3301 all contain a PGP signature. It’s complicated, but basically, they have unique cryptographic data that proves the same person(s) published the real data.
Section 5 of 5:
### The Current Status of Cicada 3301
Download Article
1
No new puzzles have been released since 2014. Fans were expecting a new puzzle in 2015, but nothing came. There are two theories regarding why the puzzles stopped appearing, but nothing has been confirmed.
Theory #1: The unsolved puzzle needs to be solved first. A lot of people have concluded that a new puzzle won’t drop until the Liber Primus puzzle is completed. This is supported by the fact that the puzzles before that one came out after the previous one had been solved.
Theory #2: 3301 have completed the competition. Another theory is that 3301 was really just recruiting people for a project (as they said) and that enough people solved the first two puzzles that they’ve recruited everyone they need right now.
2
3301 have released several messages and hints, though. In 2016, Cicada 3301 was (wrongly) reported to be connected to some terrorist attacks on Planned Parenthood. They released a message on their official Twitter page that linked to an image about them not being involved. That image contained a hidden message: “You can't see the forest when you're looking at the trees.” It’s likely a hint for the 2014 puzzle. Here are all of their other communications:
The 2016 Twitter post. A Twitter post linked to this image. Nobody has figured out if this message contains anything helpful or not and if it has any puzzle element, it remains unsolved.
The 2017 IRC server post. A year later, a new user on a Cicada 3301 forum server posted a new discovery that uncovered a final message from 3301: “Beware false paths. Always verify PGP signature from 7A35090F.” This appears to just be a reminder that anyone not using the authenticated signature is a fraud.
3301 have been silent since 2017. There have been many imitators pretending to be 3301, but no PGP-confirmed messages have been shared.
