SEVEN SERMONS TO THE DEAD (SEPTEM SERMONES AD MORTUOS)
By Carl Gustav Jung

SERMO I

The dead came back from Jerusalem, where they found not what they sought. They prayed for admittance unto me and besought my teaching, and thus I taught them:

Hear Ye: I begin with nothingness. Nothingness is the same as the fullness. In the infinity full is no better than empty. The nothingness is both empty and full. As well might ye say anything else of nothing, as for instance, white it is, or black, or that it exists not, or that it exists. That which is endless and eternal hath no qualities, since it hath all qualities.

The Nothingness or Fullness we name the PLEROMA. Therein, both thinking and being cease, since the eternal and endless hath no qualities. In it there is no one, for if any one were, he would be differentiated from the Pleroma, and would possess qualities which would distinguish him as something different from the Pleroma.

In the Pleroma there is nothing and everything. It is quite fruitless to think about the Pleroma, for this would mean self-dissolution.

CREATURA is not in the Pleroma, but in itself. The Pleroma is both beginning and end of created beings. It pervadeth them, as the light of the sun everywhere pervadeth the air. Although the Pleroma pervadeth all things, yet hath created being no share thereof, just as a wholly transparent body becometh neither dark nor light through the light which pervadeth it.

We are, however, the Pleroma itself, for we are a part of the eternal and endless. But we have no share thereof, as we are from the Pleroma infinitely removed; not in space or time, but ESSENTIALLY, since we are distinguished from the Pleroma in our essence as Creatura, which is confined within time and space.

Yet because we are parts of the Pleroma, the Pleroma is also in us. Even in the smallest point is the Pleroma endless, eternal, and entire, since small and great are qualities which are contained in it. It is that nothingness which is everywhere whole and continuous. Only figuratively, therefore, do I speak of created being as part of the Pleroma. Because, actually, the Pleroma is nowhere divided, since it is nothingness. We are also the whole Pleroma, because, figuratively, the Pleroma is the smallest point in us, merely assumed, not existing, and the boundless firmament about us.

Why, then, do we speak of the Pleroma at all, since it is everything and nothing?
I speak thereof that I may begin somewhere, and also to free you from the delusion that somewhere, either without or within, there is something fixed or in some way established from the beginning. Every so-called fixed and certain thing is only relative. That alone is fixed and certain which is subject to change.

What is the origin of creation? Created beings are distinct from the Pleroma. Differentiation is the essence of Creatura. It is for this reason that created beings are differentiated. Whence also man differeth, his essence being differentiation. If he differ not, he falleth into the Pleroma, into nothingness.

The natural striving of Creatura is toward differentiation, fight against the primeval, perilous sameness. This is called the PRINCIPIUM INDIVIDUATIONIS. This principle is the essence of Creatura. When we strive after sameness, we strive toward the Pleroma, and this is death.

SERMO II

In the night the dead stood along the wall and cried: "We would have knowledge of god. Where is god? Is god dead?"

God is not dead. Now, as ever, he liveth. God is Creatura, for he is something definite, and therefore distinct from the Pleroma. God is an active quality of the Pleroma, and all that we have said concerning Creatura is true also of him. He is distinguished from Creatura, however, in that he is much more indefinite and indeterminable than they. He is less distinct than created beings, since the ground of his being is effective fullness. Only in so far as he is definite and distinct is he Creatura, and in so far is he the manifestation of the effective fullness of the Pleroma.

Everything which we do not differentiate falleth into the Pleroma and is cancelled together with its opposite. If, therefore, we do not differentiate god, effective fullness is for us extinguished.
God is also the Pleroma itself, even as the smallest point in created beings, and also in the uncreated beings, is the Pleroma itself.

Effective emptiness is the domain of the Devil. God and Devil are the first manifestations of nothingness, which we call the Pleroma. It is indifferent whether the Pleroma is, or is not, since in all things it is balanced and cancelled. Not so Creatura. In so far as God and Devil are Creatura, they do not extinguish each other, but stand one against the other as effective contraries. We need no proof of their existence; it is enough that we must always speak of them. Even if both were not, Creatura, out of its own essence of differentiation, would produce them anew out of the Pleroma.

Everything that differentiation teareth forth from the Pleroma is a pair of opposites, to him therefore god belongeth always the devil. This inseparability is as close and, as your life hath made you see, as terrible as the Pleroma itself. For these two stand so close to the Pleroma that in both of them all opposites are cancelled and united.

God and Devil are distinguished by the qualities of fullness and emptiness, generation and destruction. EFFECTIVENESS is common to both. Effectiveness uniteth them. Wherefore effectiveness standeth above both; is a god above god, since in its effect it uniteth fullness and emptiness.

This is a god whom ye knew not, for mankind forgot him. We name him by his name ABRAXAS. He is more indefinite still than God and Devil.
To distinguish him from god we call god Helios, or Sun, and the devil Daemon.
Abraxas is effectiveness; nothing standeth opposed to him, but the ineffective; wherefore his effective nature unfoldeth itself freely.
The ineffective is not, therefore it resisteth not.
Abraxas standeth above the sun and above the devil.
It is the improbable probability, unreal reality.
Had the Pleroma a being, Abraxas would be its manifestation.
He is the fullness, he is the void, he is the sacred union of the contraries.
He is the creator and the destroyer, the womb and the tomb.
He is the monster of the underworld, the thousand-armed polyp, coiled wing of the serpent.
He is the brightest light of day and the deepest night of madness.
To see him is blindness;
To know him is sickness;
To worship him is death;
To fear him is wisdom;
Not to resist him is redemption.
God dwelleth behind the sun; the devil dwelleth behind the night.
What god bringeth forth out of the light, the devil sucketh into the night.
Abraxas is the world: its becoming and its passing.
Upon every gift of the sun, the devil layeth his curse.
Everything that ye ask from Helios the Sun-God begetteth an act of the Devil.
Everything that ye create with the Sun-God giveth power to the Devil.
That is holy Abraxas.
